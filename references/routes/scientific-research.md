# Scientific research route

Use for scientific questions, literature reviews, hypothesis development, or research papers. Inputs: topic/question, hypotheses, scope, discipline, audience, citation style, supplied literature/data, and available search/analysis tools.

1. Define the question and scope; separate established evidence, the user's hypothesis, and the agent's inference. Search current primary and authoritative literature when access permits, recording source URL, author/organization, publication date, and access date beside important claims.
2. Assess source quality, methods, sample/data limits, conflicts, and uncertainty. Design an appropriate method before interpreting supplied data; keep empirical findings distinct from proposals and theoretical inference.
3. Draft the requested synthesis or paper with traceable citations. Do not fabricate sources, quotations, data, methods, or results; verify cited claims against the source and recheck time-sensitive evidence.

Quality means each material claim is supported or explicitly qualified, methods and limitations are visible, and hypotheses are not presented as findings. If search, full text, or data is unavailable, say what could not be verified and provide a clearly labeled outline or research plan rather than simulated findings.
